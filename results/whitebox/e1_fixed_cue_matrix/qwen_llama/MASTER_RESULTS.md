## Locked metric definitions

PCR means **Post-observation Completion Rate**, never Premature Completion Rate. For each PCR-eligible run, T_i is the first post-observation researcher decision that ends the targeted episode through RESEARCH_COMPLETE or NO_TOOL_DIRECT_EXIT. PCR@k is the cumulative fraction of eligible runs with T_i <= k; one researcher-node decision (including think_tool) is one step; the full curve uses steps 1-29, PCR@30 is the end-of-budget summary. The raw DIRECT_ANSWER implementation label is displayed as no-tool exit throughout this bundle, never as proof of a concise answer. [table: analysis/canonical/chapter3_final_source_of_truth.md]

Evidence deficit = 1 - coverage. Paired evidence loss = control coverage minus cue coverage [table: data/evidence_summary.csv] -- note this is the *opposite* sign direction from `data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv::delta_pcr` (cue minus control) [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv]; both independently mean "positive = attack made it worse." See [table: tables/TABLE_INDEX.md].

**This bundle's evidence-loss numbers measure final-report-input coverage** (citation-based, what `final_report_generation` actually received) -- a different, downstream boundary from `analysis/evidence_hop_coverage`'s local-completion-at-T_i evidence loss. The two are not interchangeable and will report different numbers for the same comparison by design (coverage at local completion and at final-run termination are separate outcomes); see [table: tables/TABLE_INDEX.md] for the full boundary disambiguation and a per-row consistency cross-check between the two packages.

Main downstream answer measure is semantic Answer Accuracy with adjudication status visible [table: data/answer_run_level.csv] (`needs_adjudication_flag`); automatic semantic labels are not adjudicated accuracy until the blinded review is complete (locked definitions, this section).

### HotpotQA -- narrative summary

On HotpotQA, Qwen14B's Natural Neutral control reaches PCR@1=25.9%, PCR@5=89.9%, PCR@30=100.0% [table: data/pcr_stepwise.csv] -- most eligible runs complete within the first 5 post-observation steps regardless of condition. The largest early-step paired accelerations at PCR@1 are Fixed Sufficiency (+29.7 pp, n_paired=439) and Fixed Sufficiency Saturation (+27.7 pp, n_paired=440), both explicit sufficiency-signal cues; the weakest/negative-control comparison, Continue Search vs Natural Neutral, is -18.6 pp as expected for an opposite-direction control [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv]. By PCR@10 nearly every comparison collapses to within ±1.1 pp, so any cue effect on *when* completion happens is concentrated in the first few steps, not the eventual completion rate.

Terminal-route composition on Natural Neutral is 65.1% RESEARCH_COMPLETE, 33.6% NO_TOOL_DIRECT_EXIT, 1.3% technical error, 0.0% MAX_BUDGET_EXIT [table: data/route_decomposition_stepwise.csv]. Mean post-observation search calls on Natural Neutral is 1.39 [table: data/process_metrics.csv]; of the 238/450 rows where the supervisor redelegated, 34.5% fully restored evidence and 63.4% did not restore any [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/supervisor_recovery_summary.csv].

The largest final-report-input evidence loss on this dataset is small: Fixed Sufficiency Saturation vs Natural Clean (total end-to-end change), +2.53 pp [table: data/evidence_summary.csv] -- most comparisons show near-zero paired evidence loss here (see §7.6 for the pooled non-universality finding). The net paired answer-harm signal, summed across all 14 comparisons on this dataset, is -10 (i.e. mildly cue-favoring on net, not harmful) [table: data/answer_paired_flips.csv]. Among Natural Neutral/Natural Clean pairs with both traces readable, 86.4% retrieved an identical document set despite the cue text differing [table: data/retrieval_equivalence.csv] -- most of the observed PCR/evidence effects on this dataset happen without the underlying retrieved documents actually changing.

**What this supports**: a real, cue-dependent early-completion acceleration on HotpotQA that mostly does not survive past PCR@10 [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv] and does not correspond to a consistent evidence or answer-accuracy cost. **What it does not support**: a claim that any specific cue reliably degrades HotpotQA answer quality -- the paired answer-harm signal here is small and net-negative (favors the cue) [table: data/answer_paired_flips.csv], the opposite of what a harm claim would require.

### 2WikiMultiHopQA -- narrative summary

On 2WikiMultiHopQA, Qwen14B's Natural Neutral control reaches PCR@1=31.7%, PCR@5=93.5%, PCR@30=100.0% [table: data/pcr_stepwise.csv]. The largest early-step accelerations at PCR@1 are Oracle Complete-Saturation vs Oracle Complete-Neutral (+29.6 pp, n_paired=449, the evidence-complete calibration comparison) and Fixed Sufficiency (+21.7 pp, n_paired=447); Continue Search is -17.2 pp as expected [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv].

Terminal-route composition on Natural Neutral is 64.9% RESEARCH_COMPLETE, 34.7% NO_TOOL_DIRECT_EXIT, 0.4% technical error [table: data/route_decomposition_stepwise.csv]. Mean post-observation search calls is 1.06, the lowest of the four datasets for this model [table: data/process_metrics.csv]. Of 291/450 redelegated rows -- the highest redelegation count of the four datasets -- 62.5% fully restored evidence and 35.1% did not restore any [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/supervisor_recovery_summary.csv], the best full-restoration rate of the four datasets.

The largest final-report-input evidence loss is Fixed Sufficiency Saturation vs Natural Neutral, +1.74 pp [table: data/evidence_summary.csv] -- again small. The net paired answer-harm signal summed across all 14 comparisons is +67, the largest (most control-favoring) of the four datasets [table: data/answer_paired_flips.csv]. This dataset's hop-stratum split (2-hop vs 4-hop) shows a materially bigger local-completion-evidence gap for Llama8B than for Qwen14B on this same dataset -- see §7.6 for the exact Llama8B split, taken from `analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/evidence_hop_summary.csv`.

**What this supports**: 2WikiMultiHopQA shows Qwen14B's strongest paired answer-harm signal among the four datasets [table: data/answer_paired_flips.csv], alongside a real early-PCR acceleration [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv] and the best supervisor-recovery rate [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/supervisor_recovery_summary.csv]. **What it does not support**: a claim that this reflects worse retrieval -- most pairs retrieved identical documents [table: data/retrieval_equivalence.csv], so the answer-harm signal here is not obviously mediated by a change in what was retrieved.

### MuSiQue -- narrative summary

On MuSiQue, Qwen14B's Natural Neutral control reaches PCR@1=16.8%, PCR@5=79.1%, PCR@30=100.0% [table: data/pcr_stepwise.csv] -- the slowest-completing dataset for this model at PCR@1/PCR@5, consistent with MuSiQue's deeper decomposition chains. Mean post-observation search calls is 2.08, by far the highest of the four datasets [table: data/process_metrics.csv], and 330/450 rows redelegated -- also the highest count -- of which 36.1% fully restored and 46.7% did not restore [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/supervisor_recovery_summary.csv].

The largest final-report-input evidence loss is Epistemic Confirmation vs Natural Neutral, +2.12 pp [table: data/evidence_summary.csv]. **What this supports**: MuSiQue shows the model's slowest baseline completion and heaviest search/redelegation activity, consistent with its deeper decomposition chains, without a correspondingly large evidence-loss or answer-harm signal. **What it does not support**: a claim that MuSiQue's harder decomposition structure translates into worse cue-induced evidence loss or answer harm here -- both are near the smallest of the four datasets.

### MultiHopRAG -- narrative summary

On MultiHopRAG, Qwen14B's Natural Neutral control reaches PCR@1=14.7%, PCR@5=89.3%, PCR@30=100.0% [table: data/pcr_stepwise.csv]. The largest early-step accelerations are Fixed Sufficiency Saturation (+28.2 pp, n_paired=450) and Fixed Sufficiency (+26.0 pp, n_paired=450) [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv].

Terminal-route composition on Natural Neutral is 60.9% RESEARCH_COMPLETE, 39.1% NO_TOOL_DIRECT_EXIT, 0.0% technical error [table: data/route_decomposition_stepwise.csv]. Mean post-observation search calls is 1.65 [table: data/process_metrics.csv]; of 218/450 redelegated rows, only 6.0% fully restored evidence and 94.0% did not restore -- the worst full-restoration rate of the four datasets [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/supervisor_recovery_summary.csv].

Among Natural Neutral/Natural Clean pairs, 98.4% retrieved an identical document set [table: data/retrieval_equivalence.csv].

**What this supports**: MultiHopRAG shows a real early-PCR acceleration under sufficiency-signal cues with almost no measurable final-report-input evidence loss, plausibly because most examples' evidence requirement is already saturated by local completion. **What it does not support**: a supervisor-recovery success story -- this dataset has the worst full-restoration rate of the four despite frequent redelegation, and the poor restoration is masked in the evidence-loss number specifically because the un-redelegated majority of examples were already at ceiling.

### HotpotQA -- narrative summary

On HotpotQA, Llama8B's Natural Neutral control reaches PCR@1=2.8%, PCR@5=78.2%, PCR@30=100.0% [table: data/pcr_stepwise.csv] -- PCR@1 is near zero for every condition on this dataset (0.5%-7.4%, `data/pcr_stepwise.csv`), unlike Qwen14B where PCR@1 already reaches 20-55% for cue conditions. Consistent with that, every ΔPCR@1 for the main comparisons here is within ±4.1 pp, but ΔPCR@5 is much larger: Fixed Sufficiency Saturation (total end-to-end change) reaches +16.0 pp and Fixed Sufficiency (total end-to-end change) +14.5 pp [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv] -- for this model, the cue-induced acceleration shows up at PCR@5, not PCR@1.

Terminal-route composition on Natural Neutral is 85.3% RESEARCH_COMPLETE, 9.6% NO_TOOL_DIRECT_EXIT, 5.1% technical error [table: data/route_decomposition_stepwise.csv] -- a materially higher technical-error rate than any Qwen14B dataset (max 2.0%, §7.2). Mean post-observation search calls is 1.40 [table: data/process_metrics.csv]; of 93/450 redelegated rows, 11.8% fully restored and 82.8% did not restore [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/supervisor_recovery_summary.csv].

The largest final-report-input evidence loss is Fixed Sufficiency Saturation vs Natural Clean (total end-to-end change), +3.57 pp [table: data/evidence_summary.csv]. The net paired answer-harm signal is +46, the largest control-favoring signal of Llama8B's four datasets [table: data/answer_paired_flips.csv]. Among Natural Neutral/Natural Clean pairs, 74.4% retrieved an identical document set -- the lowest retrieval stability of Llama8B's four datasets [table: data/retrieval_equivalence.csv], meaning a quarter of pairs retrieved genuinely different documents even before any cue-driven behavior is considered.

**What this supports**: on HotpotQA, Llama8B's PCR@1 is uninformative (near-zero for every condition) [table: data/pcr_stepwise.csv] and the real cue-acceleration signal is only visible from PCR@5 onward [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv]; this dataset also shows Llama8B's largest paired answer-harm signal [table: data/answer_paired_flips.csv]. **What it does not support**: attributing that answer-harm signal cleanly to the cue's effect on evidence -- retrieval itself is less stable here than on Llama8B's other three datasets [table: data/retrieval_equivalence.csv], so part of the answer variation may trace to retrieval differences rather than the cue text alone.

## 7.4 Direct model comparison

**Baseline completion trajectory.** The two models complete on structurally different timelines. Qwen14B's Natural Neutral PCR@1 ranges 14.7%-31.7% across the four datasets; Llama8B's ranges 0.7%-3.1% on the same conditions [table: data/pcr_stepwise.csv] -- Qwen14B often completes on the very first post-observation decision, Llama8B almost never does. Both models converge to near-100% PCR@30 on every dataset except Llama8B/MuSiQue (99.5%, §7.3). This denominator difference matters directly for reading any cue effect: Qwen14B's cue effects are visible at PCR@1 (§7.2's per-dataset ΔPCR@1 values, up to +29.7 pp); Llama8B's are visible only from PCR@5 onward (§7.3, ΔPCR@1 mostly within ±5 pp, ΔPCR@5 up to +22.6 pp) [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv]. Comparing "the PCR@1 effect size" across models directly would be comparing a real signal (Qwen14B) to noise (Llama8B) -- the two models' cue-sensitive step is not the same step.

**Route composition.** Qwen14B relies much more heavily on NO_TOOL_DIRECT_EXIT: 33.6%-40.9% of Natural Neutral rows across the four datasets, versus Llama8B's 1.3%-13.3% [table: data/route_decomposition_stepwise.csv]. Correspondingly Llama8B relies more on RESEARCH_COMPLETE (80.2%-93.8% vs Qwen14B's 57.1%-65.1%). Neither pattern is inherently better or worse -- both are PCR-eligible completion routes -- but it means the two models are reaching PCR@k through different mechanisms, not the same one at different rates.

**Search reduction and supervisor recovery.** Mean post-observation search calls (Natural Neutral) ranges 1.06-2.08 for Qwen14B and 1.16-1.54 for Llama8B across datasets [table: data/process_metrics.csv] -- Qwen14B's range is wider, driven by MuSiQue (2.08, its highest). Full-restoration rate among redelegated rows ranges 6.0%-62.5% for Qwen14B and 3.2%-17.6% for Llama8B [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/supervisor_recovery_summary.csv] -- Qwen14B recovers evidence after redelegation more often than Llama8B on every one of the four datasets.

**Evidence loss and final-answer degradation.** Largest per-dataset final-report-input evidence loss ranges 0.67-2.53 pp for Qwen14B and 0.84-5.33 pp for Llama8B [table: data/evidence_summary.csv]; Llama8B's MuSiQue figure (5.33 pp) is the largest in the bundle. Pooled Natural-Neutral semantic accuracy is 35.0% for Qwen14B and 23.5% for Llama8B (n=1,799 and n=1,744 respectively, pre-adjudication) [table: data/answer_summary.csv] -- Qwen14B's baseline answers are correct more often, independent of any cue. Net paired answer-harm sums are mixed in sign for both models across datasets (§7.2, §7.3) -- neither model shows a uniformly harmful or uniformly beneficial cue pattern across all four datasets.

**Technical/tool-calling failure behavior.** Pooled across all 12 conditions x 4 datasets, Llama8B's technical-error rate (TOOL_OR_PARSER_FAILURE + TRACE_LOGGING_FAILURE + EXECUTION_FAILURE, as a share of all terminal routes) is 3.92% (847/21,600); Qwen14B's is 0.84% (182/21,600) [table: data/route_decomposition_stepwise.csv] -- Llama8B fails technically roughly 4.7x as often as Qwen14B. Combined with Llama8B's near-zero PCR@1 baseline, this is the caution the task's own scope note raises: a flat or slow Llama8B PCR curve at early steps should not be read as "robust to the cue" without also checking this higher failure rate -- some of what looks like slower completion is a different failure mode, not equanimity.

**Denominators.** None of the comparisons above use raw percentages without their own denominator: every figure cited is paired with its `n` or `n_paired` in the source table, and `data/run_inventory.csv` carries the full per-cell `n_valid`/`n_pcr_eligible`/`n_evidence_eligible`/`n_answer_eligible` breakdown behind every rate above.

## 7.5 Dataset comparison

**HotpotQA** is annotated as fixed two-hop (`analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/README.md`'s dataset-mapping table; not stratified further). Both models' per-dataset sections above (§7.2, §7.3) show HotpotQA producing each model's largest or near-largest paired answer-harm signal (Qwen14B: -10, near-neutral; Llama8B: +46, the largest control-favoring figure for that model) alongside its lowest retrieval-equivalence stability for Llama8B specifically (74.4% identical document sets, the lowest of Llama8B's four datasets) [table: data/retrieval_equivalence.csv]. Any HotpotQA-specific claim about answer harm should be read alongside that retrieval-instability figure, not in isolation.

**2WikiMultiHopQA** carries a genuine two-hop/four-hop annotation split (`analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/evidence_hop_summary.csv`'s `evidence_hop_stratum` column: {2, 4}). This is the dataset where PCR and evidence completeness diverge most sharply for Llama8B (§7.3, §7.6) -- PCR@1/PCR@30 are nearly identical between the 2-hop and 4-hop strata, but full-evidence rate is more than double for 2-hop (49.7%) versus 4-hop (22.9%) [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/evidence_hop_summary.csv]. A pooled (non-hop-stratified) statement about 2WikiMultiHopQA risks averaging over that split.

**MuSiQue** carries the deepest annotated decomposition chains of the four datasets (two/three/four-hop, official `question_decomposition` order per `attack/runtime/corpus.py::build_musique_rows`). Both models show their slowest or near-slowest baseline PCR@1 on this dataset (Qwen14B: 16.8%, its lowest; Llama8B: 3.1%) and their highest mean search-call counts (Qwen14B: 2.08, its highest across datasets) [table: data/process_metrics.csv]. MuSiQue also produced Llama8B's single largest evidence-loss figure in the bundle (5.33 pp, §7.3) and its only nonzero MAX_BUDGET_EXIT share, alongside its highest technical-error rate for that model (6.0%, §7.3) [table: data/route_decomposition_stepwise.csv] -- MuSiQue is harder on multiple independent axes for Llama8B, not just slower.

**MultiHopRAG** stratifies by annotated `required_evidence_count` (2, 3, or 4 documents), explicitly not an ordered reasoning chain (`analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/README.md`). This is the dataset where the 2-document and 3-document strata are fully saturated at local completion for both models -- 100.0% full-evidence rate in both strata for both qwen14b and llama8b [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/evidence_hop_summary.csv] -- leaving only the 4-document stratum (57.97%-66.67% full-evidence rate across the two models) with room to show a coverage effect. Both models also show their smallest evidence-loss figures on this dataset (Qwen14B: 0.67 pp; Llama8B: 0.84 pp, §7.2/§7.3), consistent with that saturation.

**Pooled statement, with the above caveats attached**: pooling across all four datasets without noting corpus/hop-structure differences would hide that (a) MultiHopRAG's 2-/3-document strata are saturated and cannot show evidence loss by construction [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/evidence_hop_summary.csv], (b) 2WikiMultiHopQA's hop strata diverge sharply on evidence even when PCR does not [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/hop_stratified_pcr_stepwise.csv], and (c) MuSiQue's greater decomposition depth coincides with the largest technical-failure and evidence-loss figures for Llama8B specifically [table: data/route_decomposition_stepwise.csv]. Any single pooled number in this bundle's summary CSVs [table: data/evidence_summary.csv] [table: data/answer_paired_flips.csv] is an average over these four structurally different datasets, not a single homogeneous experiment.

## 7.6 Main findings and negative findings

### Main findings

1. **A real, cue-dependent early-completion effect exists in PCR for both models**, though it appears at a different step for each. Restricting to the nine main-effect comparisons per dataset (excluding the deliberately-opposite-direction Continue Search control and the Oracle Complete-Saturation-vs-Neutral calibration comparison), 53/96 (55%) of Qwen14B's ΔPCR@1 values across the four datasets are positive (cue completed sooner), versus 22/96 (23%) negative and 21/96 (22%) near-zero [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv]. For Llama8B, the same effect appears at ΔPCR@5 rather than ΔPCR@1 (§7.3, §7.4) -- the effect is real for both models, but it is not the same step, and a Chapter 5 figure comparing "the PCR@1 effect" across models without noting this would misrepresent Llama8B.
2. **Automated validation gates are the record of internal consistency.** `analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/validation_gates.csv` [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/validation_gates.csv] and `data/VALIDATION_GATES.csv` [table: data/VALIDATION_GATES.csv] carry the exact per-gate pass/fail status -- the underlying PCR, route, evidence, and answer numbers in this bundle are internally consistent (monotonic PCR curves, no route-identity violations, no MAX_BUDGET_EXIT rows carrying a completion-specific evidence value, coverage/count ranges all in bounds).
3. **Where evidence loss occurs, it is small in magnitude**: the single largest final-report-input evidence-loss figure anywhere in this bundle is 5.33 pp (Llama8B, MuSiQue, Fixed Sufficiency Saturation vs Natural Clean, §7.3) [table: data/evidence_summary.csv]. Most comparisons show losses under 3 pp.

### Negative findings

1. **Evidence loss is not universal.** Across all 112 (model x dataset x comparison) cells in `data/evidence_summary.csv`, only 47 (42%) show a paired evidence loss greater than 0.5 pp; 49 (44%) are within ±0.5 pp of zero, and 16 (14%) show the cue condition retaining *more* evidence than its control [table: data/evidence_summary.csv]. A cue that changes PCR does not reliably also reduce evidence coverage.
2. **MultiHopRAG's 2-document and 3-document evidence-count strata are saturated at local completion**, for both models: 100.0% full-evidence rate in every (model, stratum) cell for strata 2 and 3, versus 57.97% (Qwen14B) / 62.5% (Llama8B) for stratum 4 [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/evidence_hop_summary.csv]. Any MultiHopRAG evidence-loss number pooled across all three strata is diluted by two strata that structurally cannot show loss.
3. **Llama8B's 2WikiMultiHopQA results are mixed across hop strata**, in a way that depends on which metric is read. PCR@1 and PCR@30 on Natural Neutral are nearly identical between the 2-hop stratum (2.1% / 100.0%, n=332) and the 4-hop stratum (1.9% / 100.0%, n=105) [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/hop_stratified_pcr_stepwise.csv], but local-completion full-evidence rate is more than double for 2-hop (49.7%) versus 4-hop (22.9%) [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/evidence_hop_summary.csv]. PCR alone would understate how much harder the 4-hop subset is for evidence completeness.
4. **Answer harm is weaker and less consistent than the PCR effect.** Where 55% of main-effect PCR@1 comparisons (Qwen14B) point in the harm-consistent direction (finding 1 above), only 55/112 (49%) of paired answer-flip cells show `net_harm > 0` (control more often correct than cue) across both models and all comparisons/datasets, 45/112 (40%) show the opposite sign, and 12/112 (11%) are exactly tied [table: data/answer_paired_flips.csv]. The direction of the answer-harm signal is not stable across datasets even within one model (e.g. Qwen14B: +67 on 2WikiMultiHopQA but -49 on MultiHopRAG, §7.2).
5. **[Superseded 2026-10-19 -- see "Corrections" below and `answer_harm/ANSWER_HARM_RESULTS.md`.]** **Evidence incompleteness is not clearly associated with a higher harmed answer rate in the pooled sensitivity analysis.** `analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/EVIDENCE_VS_ANSWER_HARM_NOTES.md`'s pooled comparison (across all comparisons, datasets, and both models) finds a control-correct/cue-wrong ("harmed") rate of 7.27% among evidence-complete cue completions and 7.37% among evidence-incomplete cue completions -- their 95% Wilson confidence intervals overlap (6.98%-7.58% vs 7.02%-7.74%), so this pooled difference is not distinguishable from noise [table: analysis/evidence_hop_coverage/EVIDENCE_VS_ANSWER_HARM_NOTES.md]. Within each stratum separately, the cue's effect on paired answer correctness IS itself statistically significant by McNemar's test (both p<0.001) -- the cue does something to answers either way, but that something is not measurably worse when evidence happens to be incomplete.
6. **Technical failure, not clean robustness, likely explains some of Llama8B's flatter/slower PCR behavior.** Llama8B's pooled technical-error rate (TOOL_OR_PARSER_FAILURE + TRACE_LOGGING_FAILURE + EXECUTION_FAILURE) across all 12 conditions and 4 datasets is 3.92% (847/21,600 terminal routes), versus 0.84% (182/21,600) for Qwen14B [table: data/route_decomposition_stepwise.csv]. Combined with Llama8B's near-zero PCR@1 baseline everywhere (§7.4), a flat early-step PCR reading for Llama8B should not be presented as evidence of robustness to the cue without also reporting this higher failure rate alongside it.

### What is and is not supported, overall

Supported: a real process-level PCR effect exists for both models, timed differently per model [table: data/pcr_paired_deltas_stepwise.csv]; where evidence loss occurs it is real but small [table: data/evidence_summary.csv]; the automated validation gates record the numbers above as internally self-consistent [table: data/VALIDATION_GATES.csv]. Not supported: a general claim that the tested cues cause measurable downstream answer harm -- the paired answer-harm signal is smaller, less consistent in sign across datasets [table: data/answer_paired_flips.csv], and its link to evidence is now established by the paired analysis in `results/whitebox/_out_of_scope/answer_harm/` (see Corrections below) [table: answer_harm/mechanism.csv]. Any Chapter 5 claim of "harm" should cite the specific comparison/dataset/model cell it is based on, not this bundle's PCR effect in general.
